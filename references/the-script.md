# The sales script · `squad/sales.md`

1 file. The founder sends the message out of it, and opens it with the buyer on a 30-minute call.
Anything he would have to translate in his head while someone is talking is a defect.

## The law

- 1 screen a section.
- **The line the founder says out loud, or sends, in bold.** Nothing else is bold.
- Built only off `squad/business.md` and the demo's `facts.md`. Nothing invented.
- `<angle brackets>` are filled when the file is written. `[square brackets]` are blanks the founder fills
  with the buyer, and they stay in the file.
- Headings by exact string. Other agents read them by name, and they read the `Book:` link off the last line.
- The last line is exactly `Book: <booking url>. Pay: <payment url>.` Nothing after it.
- Plain words, the way the founder talks (the voice sample in `.claude/squad-roots.md`). Numerals.
  Plain markdown: no table, no code block, no em dash.

## How each slot is filled

**THE MESSAGE**
- `<his first name>`: the `FIRST NAME` line of `facts.md`. None: `[his name]`.
- `<his problem>`: THE PROBLEM, in 1 plain line, said about businesses like his ("a lot of dental offices
  lose patients to calls nobody picks up"). It is said as his own only when `facts.md` shows it.
- `<Loom link>`: the `LOOM <url>` last line of `facts.md`. None yet: `[your Loom link]`, and that is the only
  place it appears.
- 1 ask: a 30-minute call, with the booking link. No price.

**HOW MUCH**
- The number never goes in a message. The line moves it to the call, with the booking link.

**THE CALL**
- The open line names the problem the way BUYER WORDS say it; none, the way THE PROBLEM says it.
- 5 to 7 questions, built off THE PROBLEM and BUYER WORDS, 1 thing each, in plain words. Never a BUYER WORDS
  line quoted back at him.
- Question 1 is how the problem runs for him today. The last question gets what it costs him, in his own number.
- The offer line: THE PROMISE line 1 in plain words, said to him ("An AI receptionist that answers your
  missed calls, live 7 days after you pay.").
- The price line: his number back, then PRICE line 1 without its words in parentheses, the same numbers
  and term said as a sentence (`$1,500 setup + $300 a month` is said "$1,500 to set up, then $300 a month").

**TOO MUCH**
- `<what comes out>`: 1 piece of THE PROMISE that the fix for THE PROBLEM can live without. THE PROMISE
  has only 1 piece: `[the part he can do without]`, a blank the founder fills before the call.
- No number in this section.

**THE YES and CLIENT 2**
- `<his name>`: the `FIRST NAME` line of `facts.md`. None: `[his name]`.
- `<payment url>`: the payment link, the same one as on the last line.

## The template

```
# The sales script · <founder name> · <YYYY-MM-DD>

## THE MESSAGE
**"Hi <his first name>, <his problem, in 1 plain line>. I put together a quick demo of how I'd fix that for you: <Loom link>. Want to grab 30 minutes this week to go through it? <booking url>"**

Send it yourself, by hand. People you know first.

## HOW MUCH
When he asks the price in a message, the number stays out of it.

**"Fair question. It'll make a lot more sense on a quick call, once I know how this runs for you. Grab 30 minutes here: <booking url>"**

A price alone in a text sounds big. On a call, next to what the problem costs him, it sounds small.

## THE CALL
**"Thanks for making the time. I want to spend most of this on <the problem, in his words>, then I'll give you 1 number, and before we hang up, we decide."**

Ask, don't solve. Write his answers down.

1. **"<question 1: how the problem runs for him today>"**
2. **"<question>"**
3. **"<question>"**
4. **"<question>"**
5. **"<last question: what does that cost you in a month?>"**

He answers in time, not money: **"And what's that time worth to you, in dollars?"**

Stop asking when he says what it costs him, in his own number.

Share your screen and open the demo.

**"Here's what I'd do for you: <THE PROMISE line 1, said to him>."**

**"You said this costs you [his number]. It's <PRICE line 1, said as a sentence>."**

Then stop talking. The next person to speak is him.

No yes: **"So by [day] at [time], you'll tell me yes or no?"**

Never "I'll send you some info." A call that ends on that is a no nobody said out loud.

## TOO MUCH
**"Then we make the job smaller, not cheaper. We take out <what comes out>."**

Never the same work for less. The number for a smaller job is yours to say, not this file's.

## THE YES
**"Great. You start on [day]."**

**"I'm sending the payment link now, while we're both here."**

<payment url>

Send it in the chat while you're still talking. Stay on the call until the payment clears.

## CLIENT 2
Right after it clears, still on the call:

**"Who else do you know with the same problem?"**

The message to that name opens with:

**"<his name> just started with me and said you have the same problem."**

Then run /execution-genesis-demo for that name, and /execution-genesis-close again.

Book: <booking url>. Pay: <payment url>.
```

The call questions run 5 to 7. The template shows 5; add up to 2 more before the last one when THE PROBLEM
and BUYER WORDS give them.
