# The sales document (the Close: `squad/sales.md`)

One file. This order. Nothing else. It is written at beat 1 and updated in place after every
call at beat 5, and it is never restarted, never duplicated per prospect, never confused with the offer document.

The document has two halves and they are governed differently.

- **The standing half** (WHO I SELL TO down through THE REFUSALS) is what the founder says. It
  changes only at a gate: beat 1's, or beat 5's conditional one when a real call argued with a
  line. Its price, promise and warranty are copies of `squad/business.md`, never edits of it.
- **The accumulating half** (THE OBJECTIONS LEDGER down through WHAT THE CALLS ARE ASKING FOR) is
  what the calls taught. Beat 5 writes it AUTO, no permission needed, because it is a record of
  what happened rather than a decision about what to say.

An accumulating section with nothing behind it yet is written as `(none yet, no call logged)`, so
the next read sees an intentional gap rather than a missing section.

# THE SALES DOCUMENT · `<brand>`

## WHO I SELL TO
One line, off `squad/business.md`'s WHO, said as the founder would say it to a stranger. Then the
refused buyer in one line, because naming who this is not for is what makes the first line
believable.

## THE SENTENCE
The offer document's one sentence, bold, alone. The founder's spoken version wins over the written
one when they differ; that is the point of the beat 1 gate.

## THE PROMISE
Three sentences max, straight off the offer document, including its honesty line when a time claim
exists. **Never a promise `squad/business.md` does not carry.** A promise invented here is one the
founder has to keep in real life.

## THE PROOF I USE
Two or three receipts from `squad/credibility-bank.md`, each with its mechanism, each with one
line saying which situation it fits so the right one gets picked on the call. A number without how
it happened is a claim.

Source ladder, climbed down only when a rung is empty: a client result from `## 4 · CLIENT
RESULTS, verbatim` first, the founder's own win from `## 1 · WINS, each with its mechanism`
second, said out loud as theirs. A named person appears only after that person said yes to being
named; otherwise the receipt runs de-identified. A quote from the market is not a receipt: it
proves the problem is real and proves nothing was delivered.

One receipt with its mechanism is a finished section. Never padded to hit a count.

## THE PLAN
The founder's own delivery answer, three or four steps, dated in days or weeks and never in phase
names. "Day 3 you get the first round back" is a plan; "discovery phase" is a brochure line. The
offer document carries no dates; the founder does, and no step gets a day they did not say.

Then two lines, last: **Book:** the booking link. **Pay:** the payment link. A link that does not
exist yet is written `[BOOKING LINK]` or `[PAYMENT LINK]` and repeated on the footer, and the page
does not ship carrying either one.

## THE PRICE, SAID OUT LOUD
The number off `squad/business.md`, flat, with what is included under it. Then the sentence the
founder will actually say it in, in their own words, confirmed at the beat 1 gate. Then the
comparison line, with its blank left open until a buyer fills it: "You said one customer is worth
[their number]. This costs less than [the comparison]."

A price the offer document marks `(proposed, not yet said out loud)` carries that same mark here,
and saying it to a real buyer is what removes it. Never a tier, a discount or a payment term the
offer document does not carry and the founder did not confirm.

## THE WARRANTY, AS I SAY IT (only when the offer document carries one)
The warranty word for word in substance, plus the one sentence the founder says when a buyer asks
what happens if it does not work. Only what the founder controls, never an outcome. Absent when
`squad/business.md` has no warranty section; never invented to fill the heading.

## THE RUNGS
Three, decided once so a rung gets read instead of invented under pressure. The full ladder and
its reasoning live in `references/gates-and-rungs.md`.

| Rung | What this offer drops |
|---|---|
| FULL | What the offer document sells, nothing removed |
| MIDDLE | <what the buyer supplies, the longer timeline, the fewer rounds> |
| FLOOR | <only the piece that touches money> |

**Never the same thing for less money.** Absent only when the offer is one price with nothing
honestly removable, which is rare and gets said out loud when it happens.

## THE DIAGNOSIS SET
The 11 to 14 questions for this business, in the four groups (where they are now 3 to 4, what is
broken 3 to 4, what staying broken costs 3 to 4, what good looks like 2). Written once here off
the offer document and `## BUYER LANGUAGE`, tuned to each person at beat 3, never rewritten from
scratch per call.

Beat 5 marks this section: a question that produced a cost sentence gets a tick, a question two
buyers could not answer gets replaced by the one the founder asked live that worked. The set is
the part of this document that improves fastest.

## THE REFUSALS
What the founder will never say or do on a call, off `## 3 · THE NEVER-LIST` in the bank: no
income promises, no invented numbers, no discount for the same work, no fake deadline, plus this
market's own worst habits. Read before block 4 gets written.

## THE OBJECTIONS LEDGER
One row per objection, in the buyer's own words. This is the section that makes call six better
than call one.

| The objection, their words | Calls | Source | What moved it | What did not |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The count is the evidence. **Three calls raising the same objection sends the founder back to the
Winning Offer** to rewrite the offer document; the rewrite never happens here, and never before
three. A row can also come from a reply, labeled `(reply · Marcus R · Aug 31)` in the source cell,
because an objection is evidence wherever it arrives.

## WHAT BUYERS SAID
Verbatim lines only, each with its source label in the same form the Winning Offer uses: `(sales
call · Marcus R · Aug 31)`. What the problem costs them, what they already pay for, what they
asked for that nobody sells. Never a paraphrase, never a line nobody said. These are the same
lines beat 7 appends to `squad/offer-research.md`; here they sit where the next call sheet reads
them.

## THE CALL RECORD
One row per call, newest last.

| Date | Name | G1 | G2 | G3 | G4 | G5 | Verdict | Last call's fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Gates are PASS or FAIL, never a maybe. Verdict is one of four words: closed · next step dated ·
stalled · dead. The last column says whether the previous call's one fix held, so a fix that keeps
failing becomes visible instead of forgotten.

## THE ONE FIX
One line, the single thing to do differently on the next call, replaced after every debrief and
never appended to. The one it replaces moves into the call record's last column.

## WHAT THE CALLS ARE ASKING FOR
The IDEAS blocks, deduplicated, one line each, each pointing at the call it came from. An idea
three different buyers asked for gets marked, because that is the line between a hunch and a
signal. Nothing here is a decision; the founder decides, and the 90-Day Plan reads it.

---

Footer, one small line, last in the file: `built <date> · calls logged <N> · last call <name>,
<date>`, plus any live flag (`[BOOKING LINK] not live`, `[PAYMENT LINK] not live`, `price not yet
said out loud`), plus `confirmed <date>` the moment the founder says yes at the beat 1 gate. That
stamp is the only record the gate happened, and the resume check reads it.

Formatting: plain markdown, no HTML. Tables for the ledger, the rungs and the call record. Bold
for the sentence and the price line. Headings by exact string, every run, because beat 5 writes
into them by name and a drifted heading is a section that is gone.
